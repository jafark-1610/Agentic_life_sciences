# Joint protein energy inference

The inference target is the three quadratic genotype-energy fields. The apparatus is calibrated using the known fractions of bound controls. Each binder is then fitted independently, with all of its denaturation and ligand conditions contributing to one count likelihood. The binding energies have no denaturant term. Denaturation shifts only the known folding direction.

At a single-ligand condition, the bound fraction can be written as a logistic function of log activity minus the binding energy minus `softplus(F + m*u)`. This expression permits fast derivatives while retaining the four-state physical model in competitive predictions. An orthogonalized genotype design reduces the large correlations between intercept, single-mutation, and pair terms. Profile-based and constant initializations are followed by damped Fisher-scoring steps; the best likelihood is retained. Calibration is estimated from high-depth controls rather than inferred from unknown binders.

Three physically distinct fields cannot be replaced by a single apparent dissociation-energy landscape. On the zero-offset denaturant plane, the transformation `F -> -F`, `A -> A+F`, `B -> B+F` leaves all ligand occupancies unchanged, even with both ligands present. Apo folding changes. The supplied nonzero-offset measurements and fixed positive slope break that reflection. The test suite checks both the invariance on the plane and its failure off the plane. No baseline-only reconstruction is treated as an answer to the full task. The signed condition offsets are relative to 1.0 M denaturant, and all absolute concentrations are positive.

Another difficulty is statistical. Separately inverting each measured variant's denaturation curve produces heterogeneous, asymmetric uncertainty in folding energy. Ordinary sequence regression on those latent estimates treats poorly determined energies as direct measurements. The two-stage control quantifies the resulting extrapolation errors; its reported per-variant capture diagnostics are explicitly distinguished from the residual after projection onto the sequence family. Joint count inference avoids that loss of information. The simulation truth is used for scoring, never for fitting or tuning an individual answer.

Predictions integrate both competing ligands through the partition function. The apo fraction and worst challenge occupancy enter the published manufacturing utility, and every supplied variant is considered. Candidate identities and row order are read from the input table. There is no hard-coded selected variant or genotype coefficient in the computational code.

Scientific tolerances are based on errors of the physical quantities, rather than agreement with one optimizer's coefficient vector. The primary and independent apparent-energy estimators can therefore both pass. The grader derives truth from the private simulation coefficients and accepts any estimator inside the public limits. Generated rubric descriptions use the staged metadata and answer. Scientific controls check at least threefold numerical clearance for the expert result.

The synthetic system omits higher-order energy interactions, kinetic trapping, aggregation, and clinical effects. Those omissions are stated model boundaries. The package does not establish external model difficulty: that requires fresh isolated model attempts after the scientific snapshot is frozen. Numeric fit controls, independent numerical checks, and stored-answer rejection are separate forms of evidence.
