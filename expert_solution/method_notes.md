# Mechanistic and statistical analysis

The physical pools are abundances per cell. Growth therefore removes concentration from both pools in addition to molecular destruction. Nuclear disappearance contains export as well as nuclear destruction; cytoplasmic delivery is nuclear export, while residual transcription enters the nuclear pool. The equilibrated baseline constrains cytoplasmic abundance relative to nuclear abundance and the export/destruction balance. These relationships identify the baseline synthesis amplitude without treating attenuation as complete shutdown.

The two origin standards identify the recovery and gain map directly. Its coefficients need not be separately decomposed into recovery and exposure, and forcing row normalization would change the physical observation model. Endogenous log signals are fitted jointly, using the normal lower-tail probability for a censored signal. An exact interval propagator integrates constant rates, known interval growth, and the measured interval synthesis fraction. A separate matrix-exponential construction checks this propagator.

Batched damped Gauss-Newton iterations fit each gene/culture combination from two distinct initial nuclear destruction rates. Objective values and observed measurement degrees of freedom are recorded in the execution summary. The fit is nonlinear; parameter boxes are numerical safeguards rather than a biological identification claim. The shortened time-window analysis refits its eligible signals. Parent exclusion leaves independently calibrated culture fits unchanged but recomputes parental contrasts, uncertainty and the combined discovery family.

Paired parental log-rate contrasts define the treatment effect. Student intervals and one-sided tests use the biological parent count, not the number of genes, time points or standard readings. Group effects average genes within each parent before estimating parental uncertainty. The groups are fixed transcript panels, not a random genome sample. Nuclear destruction is less precisely resolved than export and cytoplasmic destruction. The reserved-seed record reports its coverage separately rather than assuming all three parameters have equal precision.

The expected answer is a statistical analysis of noisy observations, not the simulated parameters. Public comparisons allow broad interval-dependent numerical differences and avoid exact labels in boundary zones. Simulation accuracy is checked separately against the generating effects. A particular 95% interval can miss its generating effect; neither a single interval nor a reserved-seed check is evidence of external-model difficulty. The scientific controls and isolated replay checks are also not model attempts.
